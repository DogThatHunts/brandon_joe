Brandon Joe Williams’ Legal Ideas, by Topic

Summary of Brandon Joe Williams’ legal claims and arguments across all 19 transcripts of his “Civilian Knowledge Course 3.0” (CKC 3.0) video series plus two related “City of Glendale Round 3” litigation case-breakdown videos (source: transcripts/TXT/01–19; files 17–19, the Post Course Videos 2–4, were added 2026-11-17 and are covered in their own section below the original topics). Brandon Joe is the sole speaker throughout — no host, unlike the KL_on_Crrow/KL_on_RiceRadio projects these notes are modeled on. Organized by topic rather than chronologically. This is a summary of what he said, not a legal assessment — citations are not verified here (see citations-sidecar.md for that pass). Contradictions are flagged inline with > **Contradiction:** blockquotes and rolled up at the end.

Citizenship and legal-fiction theory

Claim: America’s foundational citizenship class is “state citizenship” — reserved for free white persons who are citizens of one of the 50 states of the Union — and is entirely separate from “U.S. citizenship” / “citizen of the United States,” a class he says Congress created by the Civil Rights Act of 1866 (later folded into the 14th Amendment) specifically to protect freed slaves from Southern “Black Codes.” He argues the 14th Amendment, the incorporation doctrine, and the entire U.S.-citizen apparatus have nothing to do with state citizens, and that white citizens of a state have no rights or obligations under the 14th Amendment.

  • Support: Dred Scott v. Sandford (1857); Civil Rights Act of 1866; Slaughterhouse Cases (1873); Bradwell v. State (1873); Ex parte Knowles (Cal. 1855); Van Valkenburgh v. Brown (1872); U.S. v. Cruikshank (1876); Downes v. Bidwell (1901); Twining v. New Jersey; Strauder v. West Virginia (1880); Madden v. [Kentucky, misstated as] “Commissioner” (1940); Jones v. Temmer (1993); Hepburn & Dundas v. Ellzey (1805, John Marshall opinion).
  • Source: 01, “I was born a white citizen of Indiana and I’m currently a white citizen of California”; 02, “sovereign citizen was the correct term the whole time”; 03, “it’s all Dred Scott … everything is Dred Scott versus Sanford everywhere you go.”

Claim: The 9th Amendment is “the sovereignty amendment” and is the single biggest practical right a white state citizen has — it covers every license-requiring activity (driving, fishing, practicing law or medicine) without needing a license, because licensing exists only to grant 14th-Amendment persons privileges they don’t otherwise have.

  • Support: James Madison’s June 8, 1789 remarks on the proposed 9th Amendment; the fact that the 9th and 10th Amendments have “never been incorporated” into the 14th Amendment class.
  • Source: 02, “The Ninth Amendment is the sovereignty amendment… Doesn’t apply to non-whites”; 03, “every license under the Sun, Moon and Stars would all be under the Ninth Amendment.”

Claim: Legal fictions (corporations, trusts, sole proprietorships) were first added as “persons” under the 14th Amendment by Santa Clara County v. Southern Pacific Railroad Co. (1886), and were separately barred from ever being citizens of a state by Paul v. Virginia (1869). Before 1886 the only two classes affected by the 14th Amendment were non-whites and whites not citizens of a state (e.g. residents of D.C. or U.S. territories).

  • Source: 02, “the 14th Amendment protections were expanded in a case called Santa Clara County versus Southern Pacific Railroad Company in 1886 to include corporations.”

Claim: Every person born in the U.S. automatically has a second, separate “person” — a sole proprietorship — born at the same moment, evidenced by the short-form birth certificate (which he calls the articles of incorporation/birth certificate of the sole prop). The sole proprietorship, not the living man, is the actual “U.S. citizen,” “taxpayer,” holder of the Social Security number, and party to virtually every commercial transaction (credit cards, bank accounts, mortgages). He bases this heavily on 8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(22)(A)/(b)(3), 1401(a), and the “person means an individual or organization” definitions repeated across Title 8/26 statutes.

  • Source: 01, “That business, defined as a person in 8 USD 1101 subsection B3, is a U.S. citizen under 8 U.S.C. 1401A”; 03, “the sole proprietorship is a person that was born a u.s. national and a u.s. citizen at birth.”

Claim: A legal fiction (trust, corporation) can be “naturalized” — moved from jurisdiction to jurisdiction — by anyone, at will, under 8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(23) (“conferring of nationality … by any means whatsoever”), because a legal fiction has no physical body and so there is no authoritative way to contest where it is located.

  • Source: 06, “if I say Nation of the Amnesty Coalition, and I say 123 Purple Monkey Lane, that’s it”; 07, “I could do it. If I found out like the name of your trust, I could be like, fuck you. Your trust is in New Zealand.”

Contradiction: In file 02 he insists the 9th Amendment “doesn’t apply to non-whites” and that non-whites/non-state-citizens are permanently excluded from state citizenship absent a change of law, but in file 03 he says a non-white can simply claim to be white on a voter-registration/jury form and “just kind of see what happens,” since “the federal government can’t really even evaluate a lot of that stuff” — i.e., the racial boundary he otherwise treats as a hard legal line is, in his own telling, also an unenforceable administrative presumption that can be gamed.

Claim: Historically there were two separate state constitutions operating in parallel in California — the 1849 Constitution for white citizens of the state and the 1879 Constitution (written after the 14th Amendment) for 14th-Amendment persons/legal fictions — and he argues both are still independently in force today, with a white citizen able to invoke either the 1849 Constitution or (because Article I §21 of the 1849 document is “the Ninth Amendment for white citizens”) get far broader relief than a 14th-Amendment litigant invoking the 1879 document.

  • Source: 01, “As a resident of California, he falls under the California Constitution of 1879 … As a white citizen in California, he falls under the California Constitution of 1849.”

Sole proprietorship, trusts, and legal/beneficial ownership

Claim: The W-9 form itself proves the sole-proprietorship theory: it requires an “owner” line separate from the “business/disregarded entity” line, and requires the signer to certify they are a “U.S. person” — something a white state citizen cannot truthfully sign without committing perjury.

  • Source: 03, “Enter the owner’s name on line one and enter the business slash disregarded entity’s name on line two.”

Claim: Every item of personal property (cars, trusts) splits into “legal ownership” (the trustee/title-holder) and “beneficial ownership” (the one who enjoys the economic use), and certificates (silver certificates, certificates of title, certificates of trust) are, as a category, never the thing itself but only an “abstract” proving the real asset/title exists somewhere else — so a DMV certificate of title proves a legal-title/beneficial-title split exists between the registered owner and the state, the precise mechanics of which (who holds legal title, who holds beneficial title) he says must be established through discovery because he and his main collaborator, Joe Lustig(ka), disagree about which side is which.

  • Source: 07, “What’s a certificate of title? It means the title is someplace else”; 08, “Joe Lustica thinks that when you go to the DMV you are actually becoming the legal owner and the state is becoming the beneficial owner. I’m not sure.”

Claim: A trust indenture is a purely private contract that never needs to be shown to a bank; only a one-page “certificate of trust” needs to be produced, and (he says, as of 2024–2025) major banks now have their own certificate-of-trust templates that require only the trust’s own EIN, not the settlor’s SSN — which he treats as a direct, unacknowledged concession to pressure from the sovereignty movement.

  • Source: 07, “within probably about 60 days of me releasing this template … all these banks started to make their own certificate of trust template.”

UCC Article 3, negotiable instruments, and “infinite money”

Claim: Every bill, Federal Reserve note, check, and credit-card transaction is legally a “negotiable instrument” under UCC/CCC Article 3 — never “money” (which under Article I §10 and 31 U.S.C. 5103 must be gold or silver) — and a negotiable instrument need only satisfy one live element (UCC 3-104(a)(3), “unconditional”) because the other two statutory elements (payable-to-bearer-or-order, and payable-on-demand-or-at-a-definite-time) default to “yes” whenever the instrument is silent on them (UCC 3-205(b); UCC 3-108(a)). He calls this “the most important aspect of this entire flowchart” and the basis of all his litigation.

  • Source: 05, “since number one and number two have a built-in default, this point number three is actually the only thing the instrument actually needs to have”; 11, same point repeated as the centerpiece of his discovery requests.

Claim: Because a bill (an “unconditional order to pay”) has the same legal status as a Federal Reserve note, a recipient can endorse the original bill (“without recourse, pay to the order of bearer” or back to the issuer) and tender it back as full payment under CCC/UCC 3-311 (“accord and satisfaction by use of instrument”); the drawee has no authority under the UCC to selectively refuse a properly-endorsed instrument (CCC 3-603(b)), and California Evidence Code §§633–634 create presumptions (“an obligation delivered up to the debtor is presumed to have been paid”; “a person in possession of an order on himself … is presumed to have paid the money”) that he says shift the burden of proof onto the creditor.

  • Source: 10, “The drawer does not have the ability to selectively choose what kinds of notes or drafts he would like to accept”; 13, “An obligation delivered up to the debtor … is presumed to have been paid. What in the fuck?”

Claim: Anyone (with six friends) can open a federal credit union, get OC-10 Federal Reserve discount-window access, and then swap any negotiable instrument — including self-manufactured promissory notes or other people’s unpaid bills — for actual Federal Reserve notes under 12 U.S.C. 412, “without recourse,” so the credit union (not the original obligor) bears any loss. He describes this as the mechanism of “infinite money” and offers to personally help the first three groups that open a federal credit union.

  • Source: 05, “Without recourse, paid to the order of Quicken Loans, Inc. … I never have to pay back the Federal Reserve, but the customer now owes me for 30 years on their mortgage.”

Claim: The Federal Reserve Banks are “not federal instrumentalities” but “independent, privately owned, and locally controlled corporations,” per John L. Lewis v. United States (9th Cir. 1982, Judge Cecil Poole).

  • Source: 05; 10 (repeated as background for the Glendale litigation).

Contradiction: He repeatedly says “I don’t think I’ve ever denied” that the private motor carrier DOT registration and interstate commerce are inherently commercial activities (08), yet the entire thrust of his “driving vs. traveling” argument is that commercial vs. non-commercial is “mostly irrelevant” for anyone under the 14th Amendment because jurisdiction attaches through citizenship, not the Commerce Clause — i.e., he treats the commercial/non-commercial distinction as simultaneously decisive (for state citizens) and irrelevant (for everyone else), without reconciling why a federal registration scheme he concedes is commercial in nature would matter at all to someone he says the Commerce Clause cannot reach.

Taxes, “taxpayer,” and IRS procedure

Claim: The word “taxpayer” (26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(14), “any person subject to any internal revenue tax”) is the IRS’s central legal fiction/presumption — virtually every IRS form and the entire “Frivolous Tax Positions” page uses the word “taxpayer” as an unproven presumption, and simply denying taxpayer status (while never affirmatively claiming it) is, in his view, sufficient defense. He also argues “trade or business” (26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(26)) “includes the performance of the functions of a public office,” and that “employee” (26 U.S.C. 3401(c)) is limited to officers/ employees of the District of Columbia or federal instrumentalities — so income-tax withholding and liability apply only to persons working in or for the federal government.

  • Source: 06, “The word taxpayer, you have to actually go into the actual arguments … it’s 107 times. That’s the secret. That’s the linchpin.”

Claim: “United States” and “State,” wherever defined in federal statutes (26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(9) -(10); 18 U.S.C. 5; 4 U.S.C. 71-72), mean only the District of Columbia (plus U.S. territories/ insular possessions) — never the 50 states of the Union — and 26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(39) (“such citizen or resident shall be treated as residing in the District of Columbia”) is, in his view, proof that every U.S. citizen’s/sole proprietorship’s legal domicile is permanently the District of Columbia regardless of physical location.

  • Source: 06; repeated extensively in 10 and 11 as the basis for his litigation’s jurisdictional theory.

Claim: He abandoned his earlier (“2.0 course”) advice to file a “revocation of election” document, calling it “all horse shit” once he understood 26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(39) — because there is no “election” to be treated as residing in D.C.; it is a non-optional statutory presumption for anyone who is a U.S. citizen/resident, so there is nothing to revoke.

  • Source: 06, “I had my own revocation of election document … I had to throw it out the fucking nearest window.”

Claim: An IRS bill can be made to legally “vanish” by endorsing it back to the IRS (“I hereby authorize the campus to settle the account of the taxpayer … using the draft … sent by using Fedwire”) citing an unspecified IRM provision, combined with a W-8EXP filed on behalf of a self-created “foreign government” (per 18 U.S.C. 11 and 26 U.S.C. 892) rather than the earlier W-8BEN approach he now disfavors because of its treaty-benefits section.

  • Source: 06, full endorsement-letter walkthrough.

Foreign government, “peace,” and nation-building

Claim: Any individual or group can lawfully constitute itself as a “foreign government” under 18 U.S.C. 11 merely by being “at peace” (defined expansively, borrowing from Vattel’s Law of Nations and reinterpreting Sun Tzu’s “all warfare is based on deception” in reverse as a definition of peace: “the active dissemination of truth … devoid of any intent to deceive”) — no registration, recognition, or paperwork of any kind is required, “irrespective of recognition by the United States.” He models his own “Nation of the Amnesty Coalition” on the Sovereign Military Order of Malta and micronations such as the Republic of Molossia / “Slow Jamistan.”

  • Source: 04, full discussion; the Amnesty Coalition’s self-issued passports, license plates, and currency (“doobles”) are described as de facto lawful products of this “foreign government” status, limited only in that they lack the specific federal protections (18 U.S.C. 112, 878, 970, 1116, 1201) given to officially-recognized diplomats.

Claim: A document is only a legally false “identification document” under 18 U.S.C. 1028 if it purports to be issued “by or under the authority of” the United States government or was altered to deceive; a self-issued foreign-government passport that makes no such claim is not false merely because it isn’t registered with the Department of State.

  • Source: 04.

Driving versus traveling, licensing, and jurisdiction

Claim: The right to drive/travel is covered entirely by the 9th/10th Amendments for white state citizens (no case law needed), and the Commerce Clause (Art. I §8 cl.3) is the only route by which the federal government can reach a white state citizen’s driving at all — and even then only for interstate commercial hauling. For 14th-Amendment persons, by contrast, the Commerce Clause is irrelevant because 26 U.S.C. 7701(a)(39) gives Congress plenary jurisdiction regardless of where they physically are.

  • Source: 08, “the federal government always has plenary power over 14th amendment citizens always always always… that’s what we saw on 26 USC 7701 subsection a 39.”

Claim: A California driver’s license creates “a rebuttable presumption that the owner is a resident of California” (Cal. Vehicle Code §4000(h)), which he treats as per se incompatible with being a (non-resident) white citizen of a state — hence his rule that no “ambassador” of his nation may hold a driver’s license, and his own practice of carrying a “private motor carrier” DOT registration (a free FMCSA number) instead, on the theory that a private carrier (unlike a “common carrier”) does not carry a built-in presumption of commercial activity that the state must otherwise disprove.

  • Source: 08, full discussion, including his own two incidents of having utility power shut off and restored same day.

Claim: Jury pools in California are built from DMV, Franchise Tax Board, and voter-registration records of state tax filers, drivers, and registered voters (Cal. Civ. Proc. Code §203, §197) — all explicitly limited to “citizens of the United States” — which he treats as proof that non-driving, non-voting, non-tax-filing white state citizens cannot be compelled into that jury pool, and that if compelled to serve he would demand a jury limited to “white citizens of California who are living in the Glendale area.”

  • Source: 12.

Litigation procedure, discovery, and presumptions

Claim: “There is no such thing as an administrative process” — every pre-suit communication (letters, calls) should be understood only as evidence-gathering for an eventual complaint, because opposing parties “would rather die” than substantively respond, and the entire value of his platform is in teaching people to get into discovery as fast as possible, where (he argues) the balance of power flips because the other side must answer questions under oath they would otherwise never discuss (what a bill’s routing numbers mean, where remittance stubs are processed, who is the legal/beneficial owner of a car, etc.).

  • Source: 09, “there is no such thing as an administrative process … it’s just preparation for litigation.”

Claim: Every cause of action reduces to duty → breach → damages, and the fastest, lowest-risk way to manufacture a first lawsuit is to intentionally under-pay (or otherwise trigger late fees from) a utility company, because the resulting multi-hour power shutoff supplies “damages” without meaningful real-world cost, and the entire cycle (bill → endorse and tender → shutoff → sue) can be repeated indefinitely to build successive “rounds” of litigation (he describes already preparing a “Round 4” complaint against the City of Glendale while “Round 3” is still pending).

  • Source: 09; 12; 16.

Claim: A plaintiff should file an “unlimited” civil case (>$35,000 demanded) rather than a “limited” case (≤$35,000), because — per his own reading, cross-checked with AI — a limited case’s appeal routes only to an internal appellate division of the Superior Court, is harder to carry to the Court of Appeal, and functions as a near-irreversible waiver of the right eventually to reach the state Supreme Court; he set his own Glendale complaint at exactly $35,001 for this reason.

  • Source: 10, “you’re telling me if I do a limited case I can’t even get into the court of appeals … I’m not waiving my right to go to the supreme court.”

Claim: Under California Rules of Civil Procedure, a demand for discovery responses by mail carries an extra five days to respond, but delivery by process server does not — a distinction his own opposing counsel allegedly missed, causing a tentative ruling in his favor at a motion-to- compel hearing; separately, he learned (from the same hearing) that a single motion to compel covering requests for admission, interrogatories, and document requests together is procedurally defective — each discovery type requires its own motion, with its own reservation number and filing fee.

  • Source: 12, full tentative-order walkthrough.

Claim: Under California Evidence Code §402/“evidentiary hearing” practice, an opposing party’s evidence can be defeated in two separate stages — first by attacking the “preliminary facts” a presumption is built on (a §402 hearing), then, only if that fails, by attacking the “proffered evidence” itself (an evidentiary hearing) — giving a litigant “two times up to bat” if the sequence is used correctly rather than jumping straight to an evidentiary hearing.

  • Source: 13.

Flagged inconsistency (not a contradiction, but worth noting): He states in file 13 that the distinction between conclusive and rebuttable presumptions “I don’t think … they have” in the Federal Rules of Evidence, but does not verify this claim — it is presented as his own uncertain impression (“I would check that using your AI”), not a settled position.

Contracts, consideration, and the “practice law without a license” template

Claim: A contract requires only consideration (“something of value in the eye of the law”) and an agreement to do or not do something; a bill itself constitutes consideration/cash the moment it creates a debt obligation between two parties, regardless of whether the recipient subjectively regards it as valuable. Credit-card “1% cash back” is, in his view, legally inadequate consideration because the issuer captures ~99% of the instrument’s value.

  • Source: 07.

Claim: A “template to practice law without a license,” built from an online legal-treatise resource (citing, per his account, three unnamed cases on “the right to practice law in the state courts,” plus Corpus Juris Secundum §2 and 8 U.S.C. 1101(a)(22)(A)/1401(a)/1101(b)(3)), lets a white state citizen represent other people or legal fictions in court — including class actions — without a bar license, because bar-licensing requirements derive from the 14th Amendment’s privileges-and-immunities clause and therefore bind only 14th-Amendment persons. He filed his own amended complaint splitting himself (the natural person/plaintiff) from his sole proprietorship (a separate plaintiff) on this theory, which he says was accepted for docketing at the U.S. Supreme Court (No. 25-1221) without triggering Supreme Court Rule 9.1 (non-attorney representation of another party).

  • Source: 01, full template walkthrough.

Claim: California’s Rules of Professional Conduct are, in his framing, a “slave code” binding only on members of the State Bar (“released slaves” under the 13th/14th Amendments, bearing the title “Esquire” which he glosses as meaning “released slave”) — a white state citizen has no obligations under them, though he says he would not advise anyone to act disrespectfully or in bad faith regardless.

  • Source: 14.

Claim: Rule 4.3 (communication with an unrepresented person) can be used offensively against opposing counsel who refuse to engage in settlement talks, by documenting repeated good-faith contact attempts and then filing a State Bar complaint and/or motion for sanctions against not just the line attorney but supervising attorneys under Rule 5.1 (responsibilities of managerial/ supervisory lawyers).

  • Source: 14.

Race, the 14th Amendment, and his stated political goals

Case 15 (federal) complaint: two plaintiffs, one filing (17, 19)

Claim: The federal complaint again splits the plaintiff in two — the sole proprietorship (“BRANDON JOE WILLIAMS®”, a 14th-Amendment “citizen of Congress” and California resident) and Brandon Joe Williams the “white citizen of California” (not a resident, domiciled in California, privileges and immunities from the California Constitution of 1849 and Art. He says it is a template usable in any state, with a few state-specific swaps (e.g., the attorney-oath statute).

  • Support: Paul v. Virginia (artificial persons cannot be state citizens); Santa Clara County v. Southern Pacific (corporations added to the 14th-Amendment class); Slaughterhouse Cases, Bradwell v. State, Van Valkenburgh v. Brown, U.S. v. Anthony, Twining v. New Jersey; Ex parte Knowles (Cal. 1855); Downes v. Bidwell (“two national governments”); Hepburn & Dundas v. Ellzey and National Mutual Ins. v. Tidewater Transfer (disparity of citizenship); a pending Supreme Court cert petition (No. 25-1221) offered as evidence that a split two-plaintiff filing is docketable under Supreme Court Rule 9.1; Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 6067 (attorney oath).
  • Source: 17, “legal fictions cannot be citizens of a state”; 17, “Citizen of Congress means citizen of the legislative branch”; 17, “You can use this federal complaint anywhere in the country”.

Jury and trial structure. He demands a jury under the Seventh Amendment for himself, to be composed entirely of white citizens of California domiciled in the greater Los Angeles area, and separately a 14th-Amendment-citizen jury for the sole proprietorship; he says a trial would need to split into two trials or a bench trial, and offers his audience’s help “building” the jury. Source: 17, “the jury must be composed of 14th amendment us citizens” (sole-proprietorship paragraph); same file, “split into two separate trials with two separate juries”.

Why separate federal and state suits. He keeps the California Commercial Code causes of action in the state case and puts only federal causes of action in this one, because he believes a single federal cause of action in a state complaint lets the defendant remove the whole case to federal court. Source: 17, “I didn’t put a single federal cause of action into the state case”.

Federal courts use D.C. code. He claims all federal courts operate under 4 U.S.C. § 72, so the UCC section numbers are cited as “D.C. Code § 28:3-104” etc. in federal filings, and as CCC sections in state ones (same wording; the CCC just says “division” where the UCC says “article”). Source: 17, “operating under 4 U.S.C. 72”; 19, “federally that would be the DC Code Section 283-105”.

Amendment-by-amendment causes of action (17)

Claim: Each cause of action is assigned by amendment and by plaintiff. The sole proprietorship reaches the 1st, 4th and 5th Amendments only through the incorporation doctrine (cases: Gitlow v. New York for the 1st; Mapp v. Ohio for the 4th; Chicago, Burlington & Quincy v. City of Chicago for the 5th; Hamilton v. Regents and Cantwell v. Connecticut for free exercise) and has direct access to the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments, which he says were made for freed slaves and later for legal fictions. Brandon Joe Williams, as a white citizen of California, claims the Bill of Rights directly, including the 3rd, 7th, 9th and 10th, which he says were never incorporated. The 9th Amendment is called “the sovereignty amendment”: it covers every unenumerated right (driving, fishing, practicing law, having a business or a bank account) and is where the phrase “sovereign citizen” comes from, and licensing requirements of any kind apply only to 14th-Amendment citizens. The power-shutoff facts (about seven hours on January 13) are mapped onto each.

  • Support: the cases above; Dred Scott v. Sandford (Taney’s “members of the sovereignty” language, which he defends as the correct reading); 18 U.S.C. § 1595 for a civil peonage/forced-labor claim (“private right of action”); 42 U.S.C. §§ 1981–1983 as statutes he says came from the 1866 Civil Rights Act and belong only to the sole proprietorship; 4 U.S.C. § 72; Federal Rule of Evidence 301 (the defendant may rebut that the drafts are negotiable, which he says “seems like an impossible fact to rebut”).
  • Source: 17, “The Ninth Amendment is essentially The sovereignty amendment”; same file, “Sol Prop has direct access to the 13th, 14th and 15th Amendments”; same file, “private right of action in a civil case for the statutory avenue of relief is available via 18 U.S.C. 1595”.

Contradiction: He says “Sol Prop” is a mere vehicle with no flesh body (“he is unable to negotiate notes and drafts”) and so the white citizen must endorse the drafts, yet the sole proprietorship is also a plaintiff with its own constitutional causes of action and its own damages. Compare 17, “SolProp has no physical flesh body” with the same file’s “SolProp has suffered actual damages”.

Relief. He asks for $50,000: payable in legal tender (31 U.S.C. § 5103) on the sole proprietorship’s claims, and in gold and silver coin (Art. Promissory-note template and four methods of payment (18)

Claim: There are four ways to “perform” a bill: (1) the ordinary route — Federal Reserve notes, checks, or a credit-card transaction, which he says is the user generating a new unconditional promise to pay as representative of the sole proprietorship, the “issuer” (UCC 3-105); (2) endorse the draft the creditor sent and return it as payment (the flowchart already used in the Round 3 and Round 5 complaints); (3) the new template — issue a brand-new promissory note yourself, with no credit card or bank; (4) a route he found only while building the flowchart: simply dishonor the creditor’s draft without accepting it, so that UCC 3-414(b) makes the drawer liable to pay it. He presents (3) as the new contribution of this video and says (4) is “above my pay grade.”

  • Support: Art. Case 15 docket: motion to dismiss, magistrate’s recommendation, objections (19)

Claim: The City’s motion to dismiss argued (1) the whole complaint is barred by res judicata and collateral estoppel because three earlier suits (2024 federal; small claims; Round 3 state case) covered the same self-stamped bills, and (2) no claim is supported by the facts. The magistrate judge’s report and recommendation (Nov. 5) recommends dismissal without leave to amend, construes the two plaintiffs as one for res judicata purposes because “a sole proprietorship has no legal existence apart from its owner,” and describes the case as a § 1983 civil-rights case. He filed objections on Nov. 14 (deadline Nov. 19); the City has 14 days to respond; the district judge then decides.

His response. His opposition and objections argue: (a) res judicata fails on the parties — the white citizen was first added in the state case’s amended complaint and was never a party before, and a sole proprietorship cannot be a citizen of a state (Paul v. Flagged contradictions (roll-up)

  1. Race as legal fact vs. unenforceable presumption. The 9th Amendment / state citizenship is described as categorically unavailable to non-whites (02), yet elsewhere he concedes a non-white can simply assert whiteness on official forms because “the federal government can’t really even evaluate” bloodline (03).
  2. Commercial activity as both decisive and irrelevant. The commercial/non-commercial distinction is the entire basis of the Commerce Clause argument for state citizens, but is called “mostly irrelevant” for everyone else, including for the very private-motor-carrier registration he recommends, which he concedes is “ultimately … commercial in nature” (08).
  3. Race-neutral structure vs. racial sorting mechanism. The state-citizen/14th-Amendment split is described in the same video both as a race-neutral legal/historical fact and as the basis for a proposed post-repeal regime with explicitly racial admission criteria and consequences (16).
  4. Self-correction without acknowledging prior error’s scope. He repeatedly announces that prior courses (1.0, 2.0) and his own earlier advice (four- and five-star passports, “revocation of election,” “non-citizen national” status, calling himself a “sovereign citizen” an oxymoron) were “all horse shit,” while continuing to recommend some structurally similar devices (e.g., self-naturalizing trusts into foreign jurisdictions at will) without flagging the parallel.
  5. “Research is done” vs. open core questions (17–19). “Research is done pretty much” and “I don’t really think there’s going to be a 4.0” (19) versus repeated admissions of unresolved core mechanics in the same and preceding videos (18, “this one is above my pay grade”).
  6. State citizenship as white-only vs. Black state citizens after Dred Scott. The earlier “state citizen = free white person” framing (02) against his new concession that four states still treated Black residents as state citizens (19, “which still permitted Negroes to be citizens of a state”).
  7. The sole proprietorship as vehicle vs. as plaintiff with its own damages and rights. (17, “SolProp has no physical flesh body” against “SolProp has suffered actual damages”).
  8. Revised advice without reconciling the earlier course. Terminology (“natural person”) and the small-claims-first strategy are both walked back in 18 while earlier episodes presented them as settled.
